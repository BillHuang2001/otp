# `beam_rt::port` — the runtime's A.4 port layer (`port.rs` provider · `driver.rs` host · the `ops.rs` side tables)
## Intent
The runtime side of contract A.4.
`port.rs` is the **port provider**: `open_port/2`'s `Name`/`Options` decoding, the `PortRegistry`, the port-task round and the `{Port,{data,..}}` pump behind `beam_io`.
Each child module owns one post-`open_port/2` fact over the single `PortExtras` side table: `ops.rs` the table-entry operations, `exit.rs` the `'EXIT'`/`'DOWN'` a close owes, `failure.rs` the driver's own close request, `info_extras.rs` the derived `port_info/2` items.
Four are driver→owner message channels: `completion.rs` (`{inet_reply,…}`/`{inet_async,…}` — what makes `prim_inet:send/4` return), `active_input.rs` (`{tcp,…}`/`{udp,…}`/`{tcp_passive,…}`), `peer_closed.rs` (`{tcp_closed,S}`) and `inet_opts.rs` (the option mirror an accepted socket inherits).
`driver.rs` is the runtime's **own** driver C-ABI host for `{spawn_driver,Name}` ports over `beam_abi`'s frozen `DriverHost` seam; `watch.rs` is the delivery half of `os:set_signal/2`, `tty.rs` the same half of `prim_tty:tty_select/2`'s `enif_select` interest.
It is **separate** from `beam_io`'s own host layer (`beam_io::abi`), so end-to-end `.so`-driver traffic goes through this tree.
## API Surface
### `port.rs` — the `PortHook` provider (A.4)
Public: `PORT_INFO_ATOMS`, `PortName{Spawn,SpawnExecutable,Fd,SpawnDriver,SpawnDriverBuiltin}`, `StreamType{List,Binary}` (list default), `IoCounters` (behind `erlang:statistics(io)`), `PortEvent`, and `RuntimePorts` — the `beam_bifs::PortHook` impl (`open_port/2`, `port_close/1`, `port_info/1,2`) over `Rc<RefCell<PortRegistry>>` + the shared `Rc<AtomTable>` + the owning `ProcessId`.
`PortEvent::{Data,Line,Exited,Term,Completion,ActiveInput,Signal,Select}`; `PortEvent::port()` is the **one** port-bearing roster — `Data`/`Line`/`Exited`/`Term` → `Some`, the rest → `None` (`Completion`/`ActiveInput` already resolved their recipient).
`PortRegistry` offers `new()` (anchors the `driver_*` codegen unit and builds the **one** `beam_io::DriverRegistry::with_static_spawn_drivers` table), the queries (`is_live`, `port_control`, `has_driver_control`, `is_busy`/`soft_busy`, `is_empty`/`has_nothing_to_poll`/`awaits_port_reply`, `has_pending_driver_atoms`, `io_counters`, `ids`), the commands (`write_command`, `close`, `create_static_spawn_driver`, `refresh_liveness`, `arm_tty_select`) and the private pass (`register`, `pump(timeout) -> Vec<PortEvent>`, the `take_*` harvests, the line helpers, `info`, `fold_notifications`, `drain_pending_events`, `report_pending_closes`, `drain_io_completions`, `closed_snapshot`).
`ProcessLiveness` (crate-internal) is the latest **process-liveness observation**, behind `driver_monitor_process`'s positive `ERL_DRV_MONITOR_NOT_ALIVE`; `is_alive(pid) -> Option<bool>` is `try_borrow`-based because it runs *from* a driver callback.
`refresh_liveness(&ProcessTable)` republishes it from `table.pids()` at the start of every round, gated on a live `{spawn_driver,Name}` port, so a callback reached **before any round** sees `None` and `monitor_process_result` installs the monitor anyway — never a fabricated `NotAlive`.
**Static emulator-driver resolution.** `PortName::SpawnDriver` resolves the name against `create_static_spawn_driver` **before** any `dlopen` (`io.c:599-628`), and the membership test is `beam_io`'s static set (`driver::is_static_driver` over `STATIC_SPAWN_DRIVERS`, the C's `driver_tab.c` entries) — **not** "is a factory registered here", so `fd`/`efile` and an invented name take the `dlopen` route and end as the oracle's `badarg`.
A factory failure surfaces its typed error, never a fabricated port.
`PortName::bytes_from_driver_output` routes the byte paths: a driver port's reply reaches `pending_events` as an ordinary `PortNotification::Data` from the **port-task round** (`Port::take_input_notifications`, one notification per `driver_output*` call), never by a pre-round harvest off `beam_io`'s flat queue.
`PortMeta::has_driver_control` answers the driver-**`control`-capability** question behind the `port_control` gate from the bit `open_port` captures off the driver object; `spawn`/`spawn_executable`/`fd` are `false` by `Name` form, and `PortRegistry::port_control(id, op, arg)` forwards the verb **verbatim** to `beam_io` (`beam_io/src/registry.rs:357`, which reserves `"close"`).
Two emptiness predicates: `is_empty()` counts **ports only** (`system_info(port_count)`), while `has_nothing_to_poll()` is the **nothing-pending** predicate — no live port, no retained event a round harvested but no pump reported (`pending_events`), no close's `'EXIT'`/`'DOWN'` still owed (`pending_port_signals`), no armed `tty_select/2` interest and no fired-undrained `os:set_signal/2` self-pipe.
An **armed but idle** `handle` disposition is **not** pending work, so a runtime that only handles a signal and never opened a port is quiescent and pays no sleep.
`pump` syncs the signal self-pipe into the poller, drains the retained events of earlier rounds, the queued driver output/terms, any due exit status and any handled signal, and only then — when a real wake source exists (a live port or an armed `tty_select/2` interest) — polls in `PORT_POLL_SLICE` (25 ms) slices clamped by the earliest driver deadline, reporting each `driver_output` as `PortEvent::{Data,Line,Exited,Term,Signal,Select}` with the sentinel `SIGNAL_KEY` readiness filtered out.
Its early return is **after** the unconditional `take_signals` harvest and mints the outstanding close reports (`report_pending_closes` — what retires the port's number and releases its signals), and right after every round it takes the due `exit_status`s and then drains the **line channel** (`flush_line_stream_ends`' `take_eof` → `Port::flush_linebuf`, the C's `driver_eof`, then `take_line_notifications`).
A reaped child's `{exit_status,S}` must precede the flush of its own trailing partial line and the two can land in **adjacent** rounds, so `hold_or_report_line` queues a frame on `PortMeta::held_lines` while the port still `owes_exit_status()`, and `release_held_lines` drains it (FIFO, port-number order, exactly once) once the status is taken or `EXIT_STATUS_FLUSH_GRACE` (1 s) expires (`sys_drivers.c:1230-1252`).
`SpawnDriver` is the **single** OS-child implementation; this crate owns only `open_port/2`'s decoding into a term-free `SpawnSpec`/`SpawnOptions`, plus `PortMeta`.
`deliver_port_messages` delivers each event into the **owning** mailbox through `deliver_in_table`, so a blocked receiver is woken: data/exit-status to `owner`, a driver-emitted term to `to.unwrap_or(owner)`, a `{select,Handle,Ref,ready_input}` to the arming process and `{notify,Name}` (sender `system`) to `whereis(erl_signal_server)`; `close` calls `beam_io`'s `notify_port_exit(…, Closed)` **before** `registry.close(...)`.
**The retained-events lane.** A port-task round is **destructive** (`execute_port_tasks` drains every live port's buffered input into `PortNotification::Data`), so the one round outside a pump — `write_command`'s, where a **built-in** driver writes its reply from that very `output` call — folds through the **single** `fold_notifications` helper the pump also uses, and the next `pump` reports them first via `drain_pending_events`.
Delivery stays **exactly once**: the port-task round is the bytes' single producer, `drain_pending_events` `mem::take`s the buffer as it reports it, and `close` purges the closed port's retained events so a **reused** number inherits nothing.
`fold_notifications` credits a payload in **both** places at the one harvest instant (the node-global `IoCounters::note_input` and the port's own `PortMeta::input`), while `output` is credited in `write_command` at the write instant.
The **read path** is one `{Port,{data,Data}}` per driver read, rendered from `PortEvent::Data { port, read, stream, shape, owner }` by `RuntimeContext::port_data_message_shaped` → `make_stream_payload`.
`Data` is the read's **own** `hbuf`/`buf` split (`beam_io`'s `ReadMessage { header, body }`, the C's `driver_output2` operands): on a `[binary]` stream a body-only read is the bare `binary` at whatever width the child wrote (up to `READ_CHUNK`, 64 KiB, on the pointer-stable `eval_heap`), both halves the improper `[H1..Hh|<<Body>>]`, no body (`body: None`) the proper `[H1..Hh]`, and a header-less read of nothing `[]`; a `[list]` stream is the flat int list of `ReadMessage::flattened()`.
`StreamShape` carries the one producer fact a read cannot — `Data` (the read's own split decides) for every producer but a built-in driver port, `DriverOutput` for one (see Known Issues).
A **`{packet,N}`** port (`N` ∈ `{1,2,4}`, `packet.rs`) is **framed by the emulator**, for the spawn/spawn_executable/fd family only: `write_command` prepends the big-endian length header (`PacketFraming::frame_output`) and `fold_notifications` reassembles whole packets (`PacketFraming::push`), so the owner gets one message per complete packet, header stripped, out of one read or across several, both counters counting **payload** bytes only.
A driver port carries `PortMeta::packet = None` (the option belongs to its driver); a partial packet at end of stream is **dropped**, and a `{packet,1}` payload past 255 bytes or a `{packet,2}` past 65535 is a typed `unsupported` where the C kills the port with `einval`.
**The read path never blocks on the child**: a spawn/spawn_executable/fd port's stdout is `O_NONBLOCK`ed before it reaches the poller (`beam_io/src/drivers/spawn.rs:791-795`; `{fd,N}` at `:730`) and registered `Interest::READABLE` (`:1088-1090`), so `SpawnDriver::ready_input` (`:1105-1137`) loops `read` in `READ_CHUNK` (64 KiB, `:179`) blocks only while bytes come back and returns on `EAGAIN`, and `PacketFraming::push` `break`s while `rest.len() - width < length` (`packet.rs:192-194`).
**The boot's `inet_gethost` child is that shape**: `lib/kernel/src/inet_gethost_native.erl:416` opens `[overlapped_io,{args,["4"]},{packet,4},eof,binary]`, every one of which `RuntimePorts::port_options` accepts, so the Erlang side's retry-without-`overlapped_io` fallback never fires.
Three consequences: `eof` is inert (`{Port,eof}` is never synthesized; `StreamOptions::eof` is `self.line.is_some()`), no `exit_status` option ⇒ `notify_exit == false` ⇒ `take_due_exit_statuses` never polls it, and a **live but idle** child port keeps `has_nothing_to_poll()` false, so a scheduler round that ran no slice still polls it — a zero-timeout poll for the boot drive, whose `TimerWait::ImminentWithin` policy is gated on `PortRegistry::awaits_port_reply` (see *Measured*).
A **`line`/`{line,N}`** port delivers the **framed** shape through `port_line_message` (`io.c:3454`) — `{Port,{data,{eol,Data}}}` for a newline-terminated frame, `{Port,{data,{noeol,Data}}}` for a length-limited chunk or the trailing partial line released at end-of-stream — an **empty payload being a real frame** (`{eol,[]}`), with `input`/`IoCounters` bumping per frame.
A frame is a `ReadMessage` whose **body** is the frame's bytes and whose **header** is the prefix of the read that produced it (`PortEvent::Line`'s `header`), so `driver_output2("HDR",3,"a\nb\n",4)` yields two frames each consing `"HDR"`; the header is charged only as its frames' content bytes, where the C counts `hlen + len` once per call (the `beam_io` seam ask, Handoffs).
The child's **stderr is inherited** by default (never port data); `stderr_to_stdout` duplicates descriptor 1 onto 2, so its diagnostics arrive as port data in true write order (`sys/unix/sys_drivers.c:500`).
### `port/completion.rs` — the driver→owner **completion channel** (A.4)
A socket driver cannot answer a `port_command/2` (nor a queued async op) in a control op's return value: the C *sends a message* to the owner.
`completion.rs` turns `beam_io::PortCompletion` (`beam_io/src/completion.rs:289`) into `InetReply` → `{inet_reply,…}` and `InetAsync` → `{inet_async,…}`, which is what makes `prim_inet:send/4` and its siblings (blocked in `receive {inet_reply,S,Status,Mref}`) return.
`CompletionPacket { port, owner, stream, event }` (`:105`) is term-free and resolves recipient and stream at **drain** time, because a completion staged around a teardown outlives its `PortMeta` (the C's `async_error_am_all`); `ClosedPortSnapshot { port, owner, stream, line }` (`:129`) is taken by `close` **before** `meta.remove` and consulted only for the number closed, so a recycled number inherits nothing.
`PortEvent::Completion { packet }` rides `pending_events`, so `has_nothing_to_poll` covers it and both delivery routes keep exhaustive matches (`Runtime::deliver_port_messages`; `RuntimeContext::poll_port_messages`, which takes only a completion addressed to itself).
**One drain entry point.** `PortRegistry::drain_io_completions(fallback)` (`:175`) composes `drain_failures()` **first** (the driver-closed-its-own-port channel), folds every `ClosedPortSnapshot` that drain answered plus the caller's `fallback` into one list and sweeps them, so an operation a teardown answered through `async_error_am_all` is attributed through the snapshot of the port the driver closed — which is why the failure channel needs no drain row of its own.
`beam_io` keeps a completion on its port until drained, so a drain sees everything any earlier round staged, each delivered exactly once.
| drained at | `src/port.rs` | why a completion is staged there |
|---|---|---|
| `pump`'s round, right after `execute_port_tasks` | `:1811` | the task (or the `output`/`control` it runs) stages it |
| `pump`'s "nothing live to poll" early return, after `report_pending_closes` | `:1884` | minting the last port's close report answers its queued operations |
| `write_command`, inline `registry.output` (a `.so` driver) | `:1336` | the driver's own `output` stages a `gen_udp:send` reply |
| `write_command`, `execute_port_tasks` branch (a built-in) | `:1373` | the `OUTPUT` task that carries the command |
| `port_control` (defensive) | `:1445` | `prim_inet`'s `recv`/`accept` arms queue an operation that completes later |
| `close`, after `registry.close` | `:1718` | the teardown's `async_error_am_all`, harvested into a registry-owned queue |
**Rendering** — `RuntimeContext::{completion_message, inet_reply_message, inet_async_message, decode_caller_tag}`, on the pointer-stable `-eval` arena after the registry borrow drops, every atom interned in the runtime's **one shared** table:
| completion | message |
|---|---|
| `InetReply::Ok` | `{inet_reply, Port, ok}` |
| `InetReply::Error(R)` | `{inet_reply, Port, {error, R}}`, `R` from `InetReason::atom_name()` (`beam_io/src/completion.rs:125`) |
| `InetReply` + a caller tag | the 3-tuple plus **the decoded tag as a 4th element** (`inet_drv.c:2614-2637`); no tag stays 3-wide and a failed decode keeps the 3-tuple |
| `InetAsyncResult::Ok` | `{inet_async, Port, Id, ok}` |
| `InetAsyncResult::Data(read)` | `{inet_async, Port, Id, {ok, Data}}`, `Data` through `make_stream_payload(read, stream, StreamShape::Data)`, so a UDP datagram's 7-byte packed address (`wire::pack_address`) is one flat int list on `[list]` and the improper `[1,H,L,A,B,C,D\|<<Payload>>]` on `[binary]` |
| `InetAsyncResult::Port(P)` | `{inet_async, Port, Id, {ok, P}}` — the adopted descriptor's new port |
| `InetAsyncResult::Error(R)` | `{inet_async, Port, Id, {error, R}}` |
`Id` is the 16-bit id the operation's control-op reply handed out, as a small integer; `decode_caller_tag` (`:499`) decodes the `term_to_binary(Mref,[local])` image through `crate::nif::with_decode_scratch` and interns any appended name back into the live table, so a tag carrying a name cannot resolve against a scratch index the runtime does not hold.
**`Accepted { fd }` adoption.** `PortRegistry::adopt_accepted` (`:286`) is the runtime half of the C's `tcp_inet_copy` (`inet_drv.c:11834`): `beam_io::TcpInetDriver::from_socket(fd)` in the same `PortMeta` block `open_port/2`'s built-in arm builds (`PortName::SpawnDriverBuiltin`, no OS child, the driver's own `control` capability, the **listening** port's `stream`/`line` inherited), registered through `PortRegistry::register`, after which the inherited option block is installed (`data_mode(listener, stream)` first, then `seed_inet_mode` and `inherit_inet_options`) and a replay the driver rejects closes the new port and fails the accept `{error, einval}`.
The descriptor is **owned by the completion** from the moment it is staged (`beam_io` neither stores nor closes it), and every path either hands it to a driver (which closes it on teardown) or releases it through `discard_descriptor(fd)` (`:363`, `FdDriver::read_only`; a failed `register` tears its driver down) — **never leaked**.
An indescribable descriptor answers `{error, ebadf}`, a table with no port number left `{error, emfile}`, and a completion whose port has neither metadata nor a matching snapshot releases the descriptor and is dropped.
### `port/ops.rs` — the port **table** operations (`port_set_data/2`, the name registry, `port_connect/2`)
`PortExtras` (`pub(crate)`, keyed by **port number** like `meta`) holds what `open_port/2` does *not* establish: `data` (the C's `prt->data`, `erl_bif_port.c:687-760`; absent = never set, rendered `undefined`), `names` and `links` (the C's `ERTS_P_LINKS(prt)`), the `inet_opts` mirror, the `peer_closed` latches and the owner-`'EXIT'` suppression mark; `forget_port(number)` drops all of them from `PortRegistry::close`, the single place a port leaves the runtime, so a recycled number inherits nothing.
Accessors: `set_port_data`/`port_data` (`badarg` for a port the registry does not hold, `:693-695`), the name entries, `resolve_port` (the C's `lookup_port`, `:126-143`: an internal port term **or** an atom naming a registered port), `extra_links` (`None` = the port's only link is its owner), `connect_port` (the C's `set_port_connected`, `io.c:2330-2419`, non-`!` form: **adds** a link and moves `PortMeta::owner`, the one field every delivery site reads) and `suppress_owner_exit`/`owner_exit_suppressed` (`:204`/`:210`, the mark `exit.rs` reads).
The free `connect(ports, table, port, target)` is `erlang:port_connect/2`'s whole body, shared by the floor's `erlang:` spelling and the `RuntimeHooks` bridge's `erts_internal:` one, with the C's steps (`erl_bif_port.c:435-470`): resolve the port, require an internal **live** pid target (a dead one ⇒ `badarg`), then transfer — a nested registry borrow being the typed capability gap.
The `RuntimeHooks` bridge serves `port_set_data/2`/`port_get_data/1`/`erts_internal:port_connect/2`; the `ProcessHook` bridge (`../context/process_bridge.rs`) serves the name registry, consulting the **process** table first and the port registry second, so one name has one holder (`register(init, Port)` is `badarg`).
The badarg matrix is the oracle's throughout: a pid/integer/dead port as the port argument, a dead target pid, a port that already has a name, a name another entity holds, an unregistered name; `port_get_data/1` of a fresh port is `undefined`.
### `port/exit.rs` — a closing port's `'EXIT'`/`'DOWN'`
A port is linkable and monitorable, so a close owes what a dying process owes its peers: `{'EXIT',Port,Reason}` to a **trapping** link and `{'DOWN',Ref,port,Port,Reason}` to every watcher, `'EXIT'` before `'DOWN'`.
A port leaves the runtime through exactly **two** closers, both carrying a reason: `port_close/1` (`PortRegistry::close`) closes with `normal`, a driver that closes its own port supplies its own (`failure.rs`); the reason rides the signal (`PortSignal::reason: PortSignalReason`) rather than being fixed here, so one close's `'EXIT'` and `'DOWN'` can never disagree.
The policy is the link family's: a **trapping** peer gets that `'EXIT'`, a non-trapping one gets **nothing** when the reason is `normal` and is **terminated with that very reason** otherwise, every watcher gets its `'DOWN'` (outgoing `PortWatch` removed), and a recipient the table does not hold is skipped.
`PortSignalKind{Exit, Down(MonitorRef)}` + `PortSignal{port, to, kind, reason}` are per **recipient**, so a route that observes only its own mailbox takes its own entries and leaves the rest queued in `PendingPortSignals{unreported, ready}`.
The obligation is discharged in **two steps**, because `beam_process`'s `ProcessTable::port_closed` frees the port number at its first step while `beam_io` keeps it until its `PortNotification::Closed` report is minted (`take_closed_notifications` → `ids.release`), so a `close`+`open` pair in one slice cannot make a deferred report name a different live port.
`port_exit_signals(id, reason, signals_owner)` is the pure reader of that obligation (the metadata, the link set — `extra_links`, or the owner for a never-reconnected port — and the incoming monitors of the authoritative `beam_process::PortTable::monitors`, **not** the always-empty `PortMeta::monitors`); `capture_port_exit(id)` — called from `close` while all three are still readable — wraps it with `normal` and only **stashes** the snapshot, and `fold_notifications`'s `PortNotification::Closed` arm moves it into `ready`.
Its `signals_owner` argument is the owner-`'EXIT'` suppression mark: `PortExtras::no_owner_exit` makes `capture_port_exit` pass `signals_owner = false` (`exit.rs:290`), so a peer-close retirement produces the `'DOWN'`-only set the C's `driver_exit` produces (`io.c:7244` unlinks first); the mark is consumed by that one close and dropped with the rest of the port's extras.
The failure channel bypasses that second stage (`push_port_signals` → `PendingPortSignals::push_ready`), because `beam_io` has already closed *and* reported its port by the time `take_failures` answers.
Delivery is **pull-based**: `Runtime::deliver_port_signals` takes every deliverable entry (the scheduler `run` route, through `RuntimeContext::deliver_in_table`, so a parked receiver wakes) and `RuntimeContext::poll_port_signals()` only its own process's (`take_port_signals_for`, the `-eval` route); `PendingPortSignals::prune(alive)` drops an undeliverable entry, which would otherwise hold `has_nothing_to_poll()` false for ever.
### `port/failure.rs` — the driver-failure channel's runtime half (A.4)
A driver closes its **own** port through the C's `driver_failure*` family or `driver_exit` (`io.c:7196-7278`) — a **port-closing request**, not a return value.
`beam_io` latches it from inside the callback (`DriverContext::signal_failure`), runs the ordinary teardown (`reap_failures` → its `PortRegistry::close`, so the driver's `stop` runs, the port leaves the live map and its `PortNotification::Closed` report is minted) and queues the term-free `PortFailure` (`take_failures`); this module is the half `beam_io` cannot do, owning no terms and no process table.
`signal_reason(&FailureReason) -> PortSignalReason` (`pub(crate)`) is the **one** reason rule: `Eof`/`Normal` ⇒ `PortSignalReason::Normal` (not `Atom("normal")`, so the link family's *`normal` is ignored* policy applies), `Atom(name)` ⇒ that name, `Posix(errno)` ⇒ `InetReason::from_errno(..).atom_name()` (the lowercase POSIX name, never `{error, ·}`), `Code(n)` ⇒ `Integer(n)`.
`PortRegistry::drain_failures()` takes every queued failure and answers the `ClosedPortSnapshot` of each port it finished, for the completion sweep that follows; `fail_port(id, failure)` is `close`'s bookkeeping **minus** the two steps `beam_io` already performed (no `registry.close`, the port is gone; no `notify_port_exit`, the driver already saw its port exit) and otherwise runs the same helpers in the same order — `closed_snapshot`, `port_exit_signals(id, reason, failure.signals_owner)` **before** the metadata goes, `meta.remove`, `extras.forget_port`, the purge of retained events, then `push_port_signals`.
Signals are published **straight into the deliverable queue**, never stashed, because by the time `take_failures` answers `beam_io` has already closed **and** reported the port: the coincident `Closed` report finds nothing to release, so one failure yields exactly one `'EXIT'` and one `'DOWN'`.
**Delivery policy** is the C's single `erts_deliver_port_exit(prt, id, reason, 0, 0)` path, so the `'EXIT'` **and** the `'DOWN'` of one close render the same reason; `signals_owner == false` (the `driver_exit` family, `io.c:7244`) produces **no `Exit` entry at all** while every monitor still gets its `'DOWN'`, and `RuntimeContext::port_exit_reason_term` interns the atom name in the runtime's one shared table (so a `receive` clause can match it) or builds the bare small integer.
### `port/info_extras.rs` — the derived `port_info/2` items
`queue_size` is the C's `erts_port_ioq_size(prt)` (`erl_bif_info.c:3868-3874`), the driver's own staging area in both directions; `beam_io::Port` holds exactly those two queues, so the answer is `input_len() + output_len()` (a fresh port `0`).
`memory` is the C's `am_memory` arm (`:3838-3860`): the port's link nodes, monitor records, `port_set_data/2` heap and line buffer, **deliberately excluding the `Port` struct itself**, with the C's `link_size`/`monitor_size` callbacks **assigning** rather than accumulating (`:3648-3660`), so N links report one link node and N watchers one monitor half.
Its constants are the C struct sizes on a 64-bit build (`allocate_linebuf`'s `LINEBUF_INITIAL` 100, `io.c:3155-3166`); the oracle answers `48` for a bare port (one link), `90` at `{line,10}`, `180` at bare `line`, `72` with one watcher.
`monitored_by` is the C's `am_monitored_by` arm (`:3735-3765`) read from the port table's incoming monitor list — the same authority `erlang:monitor(port, Port)` writes and `Runtime::port_monitors` reports; `locking` is `port_level` for a `spawn`/`spawn_executable`/`fd` port (the C's own drivers set `ERL_DRV_FLAG_USE_PORT_LOCKING`, `sys_drivers.c:251`) and `driver_level` for every `{spawn_driver,Name}` port (see Known Issues); `parallelism` is truthfully `false`, the runtime never granting `ERTS_PTS_FLG_PARALLELISM` (`erl_bif_info.c:3895-3905`).
`awaits_port_reply` — the poll-budget predicate — is defined here too (see *Measured*), and `PortRegistry::info` chains all of these after the seven `/1` items.
### `port/driver.rs` — the driver C ABI host (A.4)
`Driver{library, host: Box<DriverPort>}` pairs a loaded driver with the host its callbacks run against (**field order is load-bearing**: the library's `Drop` runs the entry's `finish` before the host drops); `DriverPort` is the concrete `beam_abi::DriverHost` behind the opaque `ErlDrvPort_`, whose handle **is** the per-port `DriverEnv`'s address.
`load_driver(candidates, name)` delegates to `beam_abi::driver::loader::load` (a real `ErlDrvEntry` loader, distinct from the NIF loader and needing no host), tries each candidate and reports the **last** typed error; the loader appends the shared-object suffix to **every** candidate unconditionally (`erl_unix_sys_ddll.c:104-118`), so `shared_object_stem` strips one trailing `.so` first and both `…/t` and `…/t.so` resolve `…/t.so`.
`binary_to_term` (`erl_drv_binary_to_term`) decodes into a **per-thread memoized image** of the shared table (`crate::nif::with_decode_scratch`, validated in O(1) by an `AtomSnapshot` witness), records an appended name on the pending interner at its predicted index (`intern`, never `intern_atom`, since the interner's `base` is fixed when the host is built), and answers the C's `ERTS_DDT_FAIL` (`None`) for an invalid image, never a raise.
The registry hooks carry the C's codes: `add_driver_entry` → `0`, `remove_driver_entry` `1`/`0`/`-1` (`io.c:7698`), `lock_driver` off the load state (`:7324`), `create_port` = the invalid-port sentinel.
`DriverPort` owns the per-port **driver-private state** the frozen seam has no term/home for, in `driver/state.rs`: the C's `prt->ioq` (`io.c:6790-6919`) byte queue — one enqueue chunk per successful `driver_enq`/`driver_pushq` — behind `enq`/`pushq`/`deq`/`sizeq`/`peekq`, plus the `pdl_created` one-PDL guard, distinct from both `beam_io`'s Erlang→driver output queue and the port's *input* queue.
`with_port_flags` carries the port's **state** stream bits (`StreamType::port_flags` → `PORT_FLAG_BINARY` (1) for a `binary` port, else `0`), a **different** cell from the `control_flags` `set_control_flags` records (`io.c:5325-5344` vs `:5318`); `clear_driver_state` (from `stop` **and** the adapter's `Drop`) drops the queue so a late drain never reads bytes of a port that has gone.
`DriverPortAdapter` is the **port lifecycle** the host alone lacks — a `beam_io::Driver` over a loaded C driver: `start` runs the entry's `start` (the `ErlDrvPort` **is** the environment's address, in a `Box` that never moves); `output` hands the port's queued bytes to the entry's `outputv` when it declares one (**the C's preference**, `io.c:1782`/`:1597`) and to `output` otherwise, so an **`outputv`-only** entry really receives `port_command`, and queues the `driver_output` reply as the port's **input**; `stop` runs the entry's `stop` and the drop runs `finish` + `dlclose`.
An `OUTPUT` port task (`Port::run_task` → `ready_output`) **always** drains the output queue through that single `output` write path and *then* runs the entry's `ready_output` slot, when declared, as the write-**readiness** notification (`erl_port_task.c:1793-1797`); the adapter maps `load`/`name`/`state`/`selected`/`control` (the verb parsed as a `c_uint`)/`control_reply`/`notify_ready` (one **already-resolved** descriptor's `ready_input`/`ready_output`, `dispatch_ready` keeping the resolution half) plus the monitor tree, the `has_process_exit` flag and the shared `Rc<ProcessLiveness>`.
`build.rs`'s `-Wl,--export-dynamic` publishes the `driver_*`/`erl_drv_*` symbols; `open_port({spawn_driver, Name}, Options)` routes here for a name no built-in driver answers, an unknown or empty driver name being `badarg`.
**Two shared flags, seized at load.** `BusyFlag` (`driver.rs:540`) is one handle for both halves of the busy state — `busy` (a `Rc<Cell<bool>>` the host's `DriverHost::set_busy_port` writes, `io.c:5208-5264`) and `soft_busy` (a `Rc<Cell<bool>>` stamped by `DriverPortAdapter::load` from `library.entry().driver_flags`, so the bit is the **entry's**, never re-read from the library); `OsPidFlag` (`:599`) is the same pattern for the OS child pid, written by `DriverHost::set_os_pid` (the C's `port->os_pid`, `io.c:2971`) and read for `port_info(Port, os_pid)` (`erl_bif_info.c:3814-3817`).
Both are seized **before** `DriverPortAdapter::load` consumes the boxed host (`DriverPort::busy_flag`/`os_pid_flag`), because the boxed driver exposes no host afterwards; `PortMeta::driver_busy` is the runtime's handle (`port.rs:613`), the one `port_info/2` and `port_command` read, and `PortMeta::driver_os_pid` is its owed counterpart (Known Issues).
`DriverPortAdapter` overrides `beam_io::Driver`'s three capability methods from those handles — `is_busy` forwards `BusyFlag::is_busy`, so `Port::command` sees a `dlopen`ed driver's state rather than the trait default `false`; `flags` reports `DriverFlags::SOFT_BUSY` **iff** the loaded entry advertises `ERL_DRV_FLAG_SOFT_BUSY`, which is what lets `beam_io` answer the C's `[force]` `notsup` itself; `has_control` the entry's own `control != NULL`.
`DriverPort::set_os_pid` answers the C's codes: `0` when a port lifecycle has claimed the host (`control.try_borrow()` + `attached`) and `-1` (the C's invalid-port case) recording nothing otherwise; it is deliberately **not** cleared by `clear_driver_state`, because the C's `prt->os_pid` outlives the driver's private state.
`DriverPort` implements **33 of the 43** `DriverHost` hooks declared in `beam_abi/src/driver/host.rs`, all four registry slots among them, and the failure channel's three — `failure`, `port_exit` and `port_term`; the other **10** keep the frozen trait default (the C's own failure code) in three groups — the **async** pair (`r#async`, `async_port_key`), the **lifecycle** family minus `set_os_pid` (`busy_msgq_limits` / `init_ack` / `pdl_lock` / `pdl_unlock` / `pdl_inc_refc` / `pdl_dec_refc` / `pdl_get_refc`) and **`now`** — because each needs runtime structure a per-port host holds no handle to, so a C driver calling any of the 10 gets the C's failure code, never a fabricated success.
**The failure latch, and the `ERL_DRV_PORT` resolution state.** `FailureLatch` (`:643`) is the shared handle a driver's port-closing request is latched in: `set` is **first-wins** (a second request answers the C's `-1` rather than overwriting the first reason, since in the C the first call already closed the port), `take` is the drain, `is_pending` the quiescence probe, `clear` the teardown's forget.
`DriverPort::failure` maps the seam's `PortFailure` onto `beam_io::DriverFailure` (`Eof`/`Atom`/`Posix`/`Error`), flushes the driver-private `ioq` (`io.c:7220`) and answers `0`/`-1`; `DriverPort::port_exit` latches `DriverFailure::exit(code)` (`signals_owner = false`) the same way; neither can close a port, so `DriverPortAdapter::settle` drains the latch inside the lifecycle — **output first, then the request** (`DriverContext::deliver_read` refuses a read once a failure is latched), a refused hand-off **re-latched** for the next callback, and `clear_driver_state` clearing it.
`PortTerms` (`:714`) is the per-port `ERL_DRV_PORT` resolution state behind `DriverHost::port_term` (`erl_driver.h:523`), because this crate's `driver_mk_port` answers the **handle** (a `DriverEnv` address) while the term-data layer has no port table: it is stamped with the handle/`PortId` pair in `DriverPortAdapter::start`, read back by the hook and `mark_gone`'d from `clear_driver_state`, so a driver that cached its `ErlDrvPort` cannot turn it back into a dead port's id term, and an unstamped host answers `None` (the C's `ERTS_DDT_FAIL`) rather than inventing a term.
The timer family matches the C for **every** live port: the C substitutes a silent `no_timeout_callback` when the entry declares no `timeout` slot (`io.c:7601`), so `io.c:6918`'s `-1` stands only for an invalid port.
### Driver-emitted terms (`erl_drv_output_term` / `erl_drv_send_term`) — `TermQueue`
`beam_abi` decodes the driver's term-data stack machine into a real term on the host's arena and calls `DriverHost::send_term(port, to: Option<Eterm>, term)`; the C delivers there and then (`io.c:5664`), and the frozen seam gives the host no route to a receiver's mailbox, so the term is **queued** in a per-port shared `TermQueue` — `None` = the connected process (`io.c:6254`), `Some(pid)` an explicit destination (`:6289`).
A queued term's words live on the host arena, so the adapter empties the queue in both `beam_io::Driver::stop` (`io.c:6213`) and its `Drop`; `PortRegistry` seizes the handle as `meta.driver_terms` at `open_port/2`, and `take_driver_terms` surfaces each as `PortEvent::Term{port, to, term, owner}`, which the pump copies onto the pointer-stable arena (`stabilize_driver_term`) and delivers.
### `port/watch.rs` — the `os:set_signal/2` delivery half (`pub(crate)`)
One `thread_local!` `FACILITY: RefCell<FacilitySlot>` holds the facility the first `os:set_signal/2` creates and production **never drops** (a per-call facility would drop at the end of the call, and `SignalFacility::drop` restores the prior dispositions); `HANDLED: RefCell<BTreeSet<Signal>>` mirrors which signals hold `Disposition::Handle`, so `is_pollable` answers without a syscall.
`SignalWatch{token, fd}` is the poller registration: `sync(poller)` registers the facility's `read_fd()` under `SIGNAL_KEY` (= `u64::MAX - 1`) on the first round after a `handle` disposition; `is_pollable()` is the **poll-set** half and `has_pending_signal()` the **pending-work** half — a non-destructive zero-timeout readiness probe of the self-pipe, whose byte is the *whether* and the facility's private bitmask the *what*.
`crate::context::signal` owns the oracle answer surface (`{ok, OldDisposition}`/`badarg`, the kernel-only `sigstop` rule) and delegates the facility here; the message is `signal_notify_message(name)` → `(from, {notify, Name})` with `subject` interned as `system`.
### `erlang:port_command/2,3` and the port **busy state** (A.4)
The decision table lives in **`beam_io`'s `Port::command`** (`beam_io/src/port.rs:428`), reached through `PortRegistry::port_command_with` (`beam_io/src/registry.rs:470`, `port_command` beside it folding the outcome away): it answers `PortCommandOutcome::{Done,Busy}` from `PortCommandOptions::{with_force,with_nosuspend}` and the driver's own capability/state, so no caller re-derives it.
| port | options | outcome / answer | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| any | `[force]`, driver **without** `SOFT_BUSY` | `Err` = `error:notsup`, **before any output is written** | `erl_bif_port.c:195-198` |
| idle | any (incl. `[force]` with `SOFT_BUSY`) | `Done` — queued, an `OUTPUT` task scheduled; the oracle's `true` | `io.c:1763` |
| busy | `[force]` + `SOFT_BUSY` | `Done` — the busy state is bypassed | `io.c:1731`, `:1763` |
| busy | plain or `[nosuspend]` | `Busy` with **nothing queued**; `[nosuspend]` ⇒ `false`, a plain command suspends the caller | `io.c:1763-1769` |
The `[force]` refusal is a property of the driver **entry's flag word, never of the busy state**, so it is `notsup` whether or not the port is busy; a `Busy` answer deliberately leaves the bytes out of the output queue, because the oracle suspends the caller and re-issues the command when the driver clears the state (`erts_port_resume_procs`, `io.c:5265`), so queueing now would deliver it twice or out of order.
`BusyFlag`'s two cells are the state pair `Port::command` reads through `PortMeta::driver_busy`; a `spawn`/`fd` port and a **built-in** driver port carry `None`, the C's "never busy, no `SOFT_BUSY`" case.
**A built-in driver port exposes no `SOFT_BUSY`**, so `[force]` is `notsup` for `ram_file_drv`/`tcp_inet`/`udp_inet` **idle and busy** alike (measured on the oracle), and `spawn`/`spawn_executable`/`fd` ports carry the same answer.
The runtime's own `port_command` (`../native/port.rs`) still hand-rolls this table and raises the typed capability gap for a plain command on a busy port; re-pointing it at the seam is owed to `src/port.rs` (Known Issues).
## Constraints
- Crate is `#![deny(unsafe_code)]`: the registry slots operate over a **thread-local** entry list `DRIVER_ENTRIES`, not a process-global `DriverTable`, because the runtime drives every port on one thread.
- Every driver callback runs under `publishing` → `beam_abi::with_current_host` (`start`/`ready_*`/`output`/`timeout`/`stop`), which is what lets the two port-less registry slots reach a host during a callback.
- The `SignalFacility` is a `thread_local` (not a `Runtime` field) because the runtime is single-threaded (`!Send`), so the slot *is* the process's one facility.
- Never a fabricated success: failures are typed `BeamError`s and an unservable state/lifecycle returns the C's own failure code; the `Rc<RefCell<..>>` handles are the ETS pattern so a port operation never needs a runtime borrow a `fun` argument already holds — a nested borrow is a typed gap, never a panic.
- A payload is credited where it is harvested and the queue it came from is empty afterwards (`fold_notifications` + `drain_pending_events` for the data lane, `PortNotification::Closed` for the signal lane).
## Measured — the poll budget a pass hands this layer (`startup_ms`)
The largest wall-clock cost this layer can impose on a CLI start-up is **not work**: it is the *blocking budget* a zero-slice scheduler pass hands `PortRegistry::pump`; read this before touching `PORT_POLL_SLICE`, `pump`'s wait clamp, or `has_nothing_to_poll`.
- **`PORT_POLL_SLICE` is 25 ms** and doubles as *both* the poller's inner clamp and the `port_timeout` a pass that ran no slice passes in (`../runtime/scheduler.rs::scheduler_pass`), so a live-but-idle port costs a whole `epoll_wait(…, 25)` per such pass — `strace -f -w` measures five `epoll_wait(3, [], 1024, 25) = 0` at 25.07–26.2 ms each when it does.
- **The predicate that separates the two cases is `PortRegistry::awaits_port_reply`** (`info_extras.rs`) over the per-port byte counters kept for `port_info(Port, input)`/`output` (`PortMeta::{input, output}`): a port with `output > input` has bytes it has not answered, so the wait is justified, while a port that answered everything (the boot's `inet_gethost` child) cannot speak again until it is written to, so waiting on it is pure sleep.
- **The budget cannot simply be zeroed** under a blocked round: that poll resolves the kernel's `inet_gethost` round trip, and with the budget forced to zero `drive_scheduler` returns `0` on its first pump, so the `{apply,{application,start_boot,…}}` directive folds to `unsupported: opcode: run: process blocked` (exit 3 — measured); the predicate therefore *shortens* the wait only for a port that owes nothing, which is exactly the case the reply cannot be in.
- **The gate is applied** at `../runtime/scheduler.rs` (`Runtime::awaits_port_reply`): a `TimerWait::ImminentWithin(_)` pass — only the boot drive uses that policy — gets `Duration::ZERO` when the predicate is false, and stays patient when the table cannot be borrowed.
  Measured on that combination: blocking rounds **8 → 1**, `strace -f -c -w`'s `epoll_wait` total **130.8 ms → 4.1 ms** over the same **15** calls, `read` 233 ms → 104 ms, the lookup unchanged (**98** `.beam` opens and exactly one `execve` of `<erts>/bin/inet_gethost`), the CPU cost identical either side; the paired medians are in [`../PERF.md`](../PERF.md) *The boot path's one live-but-idle port, and the poll budget*.
- **The rule for any future change**: a pass may block on the poller only when something can actually answer — a port with a reply in flight, an armed driver deadline (`beam_io::PortRegistry::next_deadline`) or an armed `tty_select/2` interest; blocking merely because a port is *live* costs a slice per pass for ever.
- **Re-measure with deterministic counters, not timings**: non-zero `port_timeout` rounds per run, `strace -f -c -w`'s `epoll_wait` total, the `.beam` openat count (**98**) and `execve` of `<erts>/bin/inet_gethost` (**exactly 1**); the pinning tests are `tests.rs::a_port_awaits_a_reply_exactly_while_a_written_command_is_unanswered` and `../runtime/scheduler/tests.rs::the_port_poll_blocks_only_for_a_round_that_ran_nothing`.
## Known Issues (this is a floor, not the full C `io.c`)
- **`erlang:port_control/3` is live end to end**, behind the driver-entry capability gate (`PortRegistry::has_driver_control`): the reply renders as a **list of byte integers** at every length, and as a **binary** only once the driver set `PORT_CONTROL_FLAG_BINARY` through `set_port_control_flags` (a per-port flag persisting across calls), a `NULL` reply pointer being the C's `NIL` (bare `[]`, `io.c:4126`); the residual is a reply the driver answers from **its own** memory, the typed *unsupported* (Handoffs).
- **A NULL driver reply with `PORT_CONTROL_FLAG_BINARY` set answers `<<>>` where the C answers `[]`**: `write_port_control_result` (`io.c:4126`) returns `NIL` before it looks at the flag, while the adapter collapses "the driver left `*rbuf` NULL" and "the driver answered zero bytes" into one empty `Vec`.
- **The one `port_command/2,3` divergence: a plain command on a busy port** — the C **suspends** the caller (`erts_suspend` + `ERTS_BIF_YIELD3`, `erl_bif_port.c:211-220`) until the port goes idle, and this runtime's BIF seam has no block channel, so that arm raises the typed capability gap (exit 3, reason naming the busy state); every other arm matches the oracle.
- **A driver port can never report `{exit_status,N}`**: the only reporter is the emulator-internal `driver_report_exit` (`io.c:5533`), **not declared in `erl_driver.h`**, so no C driver can call it and `take_exit_status`'s `Ok(None)` is correct, not a gap.
- **`driver_async` schedules nothing**: `r#async` keeps the frozen default `-1`, because `Runtime` is `!Send`, no worker thread can call back into it, and the C's in-band path when the pool is empty (`erl_async.c:604-613`) needs the entry's `ready_async`, which only the adapter's loaded library holds.
- `beam_io`'s `EfileDriver`/`FdDriver` do open/read/write only and are **never constructed by this runtime** (only `SpawnDriver` is); file I/O here is the `std::fs`/`std::env` native floor under `../native/prim*.rs`.
- **Driver-interned atoms land at the port boundary, not inline**: a name first interned *inside* a callback gets a **predicted** index (the live table is borrowed for the whole callback), `commit_driver_atoms` commits it at that exact index, and a prediction the table has moved past is **reported** (`dropped`, printed), never interned at a foreign index; `has_pending_driver_atoms`'s guard runs **before** any `atoms_mut` borrow, so the shared table is deep-cloned only in a round where a driver really interned a new name (`tests.rs::an_idle_port_round_does_not_clone_the_shared_atom_table`).
- **The monitor family is live end to end**: `DriverPort` keeps the identity-keyed `DriverMonitors` tree (through the safe `beam_abi::monitor_identity`/`monitor_from_identity`), seized as `PortMeta.driver_monitors` at `open_port/2`, and `monitor_process_result` answers the C's `0`/`1`/`-1` over a five-way split (no `process_exit` slot ⇒ `-1`; non-local pid ⇒ `-1`; observed-alive local ⇒ `0` plus a fresh holder; observed-dead local ⇒ `1` with nothing installed; **no observation** ⇒ `0`, the non-regression fallback).
  `note_driver_monitor_exits(&ProcessTable)` walks every live tree and calls `note_process_exit(pid)` for each watched pid the table no longer holds; `Runtime::drain_driver_monitor_exits` **defers** with `Ok(0)` on a live borrow, and the scheduler pass runs it immediately before `deliver_port_messages`.
- **`PORT_FLAG_LINE` (2) is reachable and line framing is adopted end to end**: `open_port/2` decodes `line`/`{line,N}` into `PortOptions::line` (a bare `line` is `LINE_DEFAULT_MAX_LEN` = 512) and arms it through `beam_io`'s `set_line_mode(id, Some(max_len))`; `StreamType::port_flags(line)` ORs `PORT_FLAG_BINARY` (1) and `PORT_FLAG_LINE` (2) independently (`[line]` ⇒ 2, `[binary]` ⇒ 1, `[binary, line]` ⇒ 3, none ⇒ 0), the exclusivity rules are checked after the whole list is parsed (`line` + `{packet,N}`, `stderr_to_stdout` without `use_stdio` ⇒ `badarg`), and `stream_options` asks the child driver for its end-of-stream observation on exactly the line-mode ports.
- **`get_port_flags` is answerable from inside `start` where the oracle answers `0`**: the oracle ORs `ERTS_PORT_SFLG_BINARY_IO`/`_LINEBUF_IO` into `port->state` only **after** `open_driver` returns (`erl_bif_port.c:1139-1143`), while `with_port_flags` sets the bits at host construction; no corpus case calls it from `start`.
- **`driver_async_port_key` stays unimplemented** (`async_port_key` keeps the default `-1`): the C body (`erl_async.c:518-525`) returns the port's internal data word truncated to 32 bits, an address-derived value whose only defined use is the pool bucket selector (`:585-593`), and the seam has no handle to it.
- A driver that armed **more than one** registration per direction is a typed `unsupported` (`armed_event`), never a wrong descriptor.
- `os:set_signal/2`'s notification is **dropped when no `erl_signal_server` is registered** (`sys.c:589-604`), never an error; the `-eval` receive path drops a `PortEvent::Signal` (it observes only its own mailbox) while the scheduler path delivers it.
- **A built-in driver port's `{data, D}` renders as list elements on either stream**: a `PortName::SpawnDriverBuiltin` port reports `StreamShape::DriverOutput`, rendering its **flat, header-less** queue as list elements (`io.c:3404-3435`), so every header-only reply is the oracle's **exact** list; the residual is a **producer** fact — an in-tree driver hands its bytes up through `beam_io`'s flat `DriverContext::input()`, which cannot say which of the four `driver_output*` entry points wrote them, so `DATA`'s improper binary tail (`ram_file_drv.c:448`) renders as a proper list of the same bytes.
- **A `dlopen`ed driver's split read survives end to end**: `DriverHost::output2` records the C's `hbuf`/`hlen` + `buf`/`len` halves as `(header, Option<body>)` (`body == None` is the C's `buf == NULL`, `Some(&[])` a present empty tail) and `output` the header-less `(Vec::new(), Some(data))` shape, and `collect_outputs` hands each recorded call to `DriverContext::deliver_read` as **one** read — so `driver_output2`'s `[72,68,82|<<"abc">>]`, `driver_output_binary`'s improper tail and `driver_outputv`'s vector are oracle-identical (`driver_output_shape_tests.rs`, over the `eventsplit` fixture mode).
- **A zero-byte read produces no message**: `fold_notifications` drops a read `ReadMessage::is_empty()` reports (no header and no byte), right for the `{packet,N}` arm it precedes but leaving `driver_output2(NULL,0,NULL,0)`/`driver_output("",0)` silent where the oracle delivers `{Port,{data,[]}}`/`{Port,{data,<<>>}}`; both shapes are built by `make_stream_payload` and pinned at the builder level (`stream_shape_tests.rs`).
- **`port_set_data/2`'s term words are not counted by `port_info(Port, memory)`**: the C copies the term onto a private heap, while this runtime keeps it on its pointer-stable store, so `port_set_data(P,{foo,1})` reports `120` where the oracle reports `136`; no number is fabricated.
- **`locking` reports `driver_level` for every `{spawn_driver,Name}` port**: the C decides on the entry's `ERL_DRV_FLAG_USE_PORT_LOCKING` (`erl_bif_info.c:3875-3894`), but the bit does not cross the `beam_io` seam (`DriverFlags` keeps only `SOFT_BUSY`); the OS-child drivers report `port_level`, the oracle's own answer.
- **A tagged port monitor's `'DOWN'` carries the default head**: `erlang:monitor/3`'s `{tag, Tag}` for a **port** monitor lives in `beam_process`'s private `ProcessTable::tags`, which no API of that crate exposes; the untagged shape is byte-exact.
- **The `-eval`/action process starts with `trap_exit = false`** (`../runtime.rs::as_action_process`), where the oracle's `-eval` process reports `{trap_exit,true}`, which is why every port-exit fixture sets the flag explicitly.
- **The inet stack reaches this node end to end, and the completion channel is what closes the send path**: agreeing with the oracle, `gen_udp:open/1,2`, `gen_tcp:listen/2` (the `[binary]` form included), `inet:getstat/1,2` and a two-socket **UDP round trip** (`gen_udp:recv(A,0,5000)` ⇒ `{ok,{{127,0,0,1},Port,<<"hi">>}}`) all work because `prim_inet:send/4`'s `{inet_reply,…}` arrives, and `gen_tcp:connect/2,3,4` runs because `inet:timeout/1` reaches `erlang:read_timer/1` over `Timers::remaining` (`../timers.rs`), so a connect to a closed loopback port answers `{error,econnrefused}` and one to an in-runtime listener `ok`.
  Two residuals, both typed: (a) a **nested blocking call on the `-eval` route** with no deadline folds to `beam_engine::error::blocked()` (exit **3**) — a `gen_udp:recv/3` with no datagram yet, any `gen_tcp:accept` — which is the `-eval` pump's inability to resume a *still* parked nested call (`../context/pump.rs`, sibling-owned), not a channel gap, and the scheduled `run` route pumps and resumes; (b) the driver's **async-op queue** (`INET_MAX_ASYNC`) is not modelled, so a control-op `recv` is a synchronous read-now substitution answering `[1]` where the oracle returns an op-id (`[1,0,0]`) and later delivers `{inet_async, Port, Ref, {ok, Data}}`.
- **A completion's recipient is the port's `owner`, not the C's `desc->caller`**: the frozen `beam_io::Driver` seam exposes no `driver_caller`, so `drain_io_completions` resolves `PortMeta::owner` (`completion.rs:67-73`); the two coincide for every `prim_inet` caller, so the divergence is unobservable in practice.
- **`{mode, list}` after `open_port/2` does not re-render a completion as a list**: `PortMeta::stream` is fixed at open time (`prim_inet:open/5` always passes `[binary]`) and a `{mode,…}` `setopts` travels as a control op this node forwards **verbatim**, so the drained packet carries that stale snapshot (`completion.rs:176-181`) for the port's whole life.
- **`port_command/2,3` and `port_control/3` take only a port term**, where the C also resolves an atom name: both decode with `PortId::from_eterm` (`beam_process/src/pid.rs:129`) → `badarg`, while the C's `lookup_port` (`erl_bif_port.c:129-141`) maps an atom through `erts_whereis_name_to_id` and the specs are `Port :: port() | atom()` (`erts/preloaded/src/erlang.erl:12006,12207`); `port_info/1,2` and `port_connect/2` **do** resolve a name, so the gap is confined to these two.
- **`driver_failure_atom/2`'s name crosses the `beam_abi` seam as UTF-8, while the C interns Latin-1 bytes**: `beam_abi/src/driver/drv/lifecycle.rs::driver_failure_atom` answers `-1` for a name that is not valid UTF-8 (the seam's reason is a `&str`), and a valid multi-byte UTF-8 name becomes **one** atom carrying those codepoints where the C interns the individual bytes; only the ASCII rows are oracle-identical.
- **The data-then-failure row and the non-trapping owner's fate are pinned end to end by `failure_e2e_tests.rs`**: the read a failing callback wrote reaches its owner **before** the exit signal (`fold_notifications`' `Data` arm falls back to the recipient facts `fail_port` snapshots in `failed_read_recipients`, consumed once, dropped at the port's `Closed` report and cleared on `register`), a non-trapping owner is terminated for a non-`normal` reason and survives `normal`, and `driver_exit` never signals a linked owner.
- **`{udp_closed,_}` does not exist in the oracle**, so a `udp_inet` port never reports a peer close and none is ever synthesized (see `peer_closed.rs` below); a connected UDP socket's ICMP failure is `{udp_error,S,econnrefused}`, a different family.
- **Owed to `src/port.rs` (out of this node's write scope), five items.** (1) `PortMeta::driver_os_pid: Option<driver::OsPidFlag>`, seized in the `PortName::SpawnDriver` arm of `open_port/2` **before** `DriverPortAdapter::load` consumes the host, plus the `PortRegistry::info` `os_pid` arm onto it (`None` ⇒ `undefined`; today that item reads the OS-child `PortMeta::os_pid`, which a driver port reports as `undefined`).
  (2) The two peer-close call sites — `deliver_peer_closes` in `deliver_port_messages`, `poll_peer_closes` in `poll_port_messages` — without which that channel is compiled but unreachable; (3) a `PortRegistry::command` wrapper over `beam_io`'s `port_command_with`, with the byte accounting moved so a **refused/Busy** command is not counted as output (`write_command` credits `IoCounters`/`PortMeta::output` before it queues); (4) `port_options`: `[exit_status]` ⇒ **`badarg`** for any port whose name resolves to a registered driver — the name is looked up in the driver list for both forms, so `{spawn,"tcp_inet"}` is covered too (`io.c:625-628`) — while `[stderr_to_stdout]` is accepted-and-inert for a driver port; (5) `../native/port.rs::port_command` must be re-pointed at that seam, replacing its hand-rolled `is_busy`/`soft_busy`/`notsup` shortcut.

## Known divergence from the oracle: no child-exit auto-close
- ERTS **auto-closes** a `{spawn, Program}` port once its child exits: after the child is reaped, `erlang:port_info(P)` is `undefined`, `lists:member(P, erlang:ports())` is `false`, and `erlang:port_close(P)` raises `error:badarg`.
- This runtime does **not** auto-close on child exit: `PortMeta.notify_exit` is only set by the `exit_status` option, so nothing reaps or removes the port until an explicit `port_close` — `close` kills+reaps, `meta.remove`s, forgets the extras and releases the id, synchronously.
- The child is therefore reaped at exactly two points, both of which **`SIGKILL` it and then block in `wait`**: `PortRegistry::close` → `beam_io` `Driver::stop` (`drivers/spawn.rs:1139-1154`) → `kill_and_reap` (`:1225-1246`), and — for a port nobody closed — the driver's `Drop` (`:1165-1175`) when the `Runtime` drops; because `main` evaluates `cli::run` and only then calls `std::process::exit` (`src/main.rs:29`), the `Runtime` in `cli::run`'s `source` **is** dropped on the `halt()` path, so the boot's idle `inet_gethost` child is killed and waited for there and a `wait4` after the last poll round belongs to that teardown, not to a round.
- The corpus **sidesteps** the divergence rather than pinning it (the five `driver/port_*` close cases each open a child alive until the explicit `port_close/1`), so the gate's 0-mismatch verdict is consistent with it.

## `port/active_input.rs` — the `{active, …}` input channel (A.4)
`beam_io`'s **active-mode input channel** is the driver→owner path of an `{active, true|once|N}` socket: such a socket never parks its input in the port's byte queue, so the driver *sends a message to the owner* from its read-readiness callback.
The driver half (`beam_io`'s `drivers/inet/active.rs`, `drivers/inet/udp/active.rs`) stages one `ActiveInput { port, event }` per delivered unit through `DriverContext::deliver_active_input`; `active_input.rs` is the runtime half, and its module docs carry the C derivation.
| event | port kind | message | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ActiveInputEvent::Data(read)` | `tcp_inet` | `{tcp, S, Data}` | `inet_drv.c:3979-4055` |
| `ActiveInputEvent::Data(read)` | `udp_inet` | `{udp, S, IP, Port, Data}` | `packet_binary_message`, `:4105-4160` |
| `ActiveInputEvent::Passive` | `tcp_inet` | `{tcp_passive, S}` | `packet_passive_message`, `:4215` |
| `ActiveInputEvent::Passive` | `udp_inet` | `{udp_passive, S}` | the same builder, the other branch |
The invariants below are pinned by `active_input_tests.rs` (16 made-up reads) and `active_input_e2e_tests.rs` (9, 8 over real loopback).
- **`ActiveInputPacket { port, owner, data_mode, kind, event }`** mirrors `CompletionPacket`: the owner, stream mode and port **kind** are resolved at **drain** time (`PortRegistry::drain_active_inputs`, from `PortMeta`), because a delivery staged around a teardown outlives the metadata; `ActivePortKind { Tcp, Udp, Other }` comes from `driver_path_name(&meta.command)`, and `Other` is a **typed capability gap** for both events, never a fabricated `{tcp,…}` and never a silent drop.
- A delivery whose port has already left the runtime is **dropped** (the completion channel's rule); the packet carries the `owner`/`port` it resolved, so nothing is re-attributed to a later port that reuses the number.
- **One delivered unit is `beam_io`'s decision**: under a framing `{packet,N}` one delivery is **one frame** with the header already stripped, under `{packet,raw}` one read burst; a read's `header` is the `{header,N}` option (`desc->hsz`) for a stream port and the 7-byte packed source address for a datagram port — never the framing header.
- **Payload rendering goes through the one stream shaper**: TCP calls `make_stream_payload(read, mode, StreamShape::Data)`; UDP decodes the address out of the read's header (`decode_packed_address`: `[INET_AF_INET(=1), port_hi, port_lo, a, b, c, d]`, port big-endian) and shapes the **body alone** from a header-less `ReadMessage`; a header that is not exactly one packed IPv4 record is a typed gap, because an address is the one element of `{udp,…}` that must never be invented.
- **The mode seam is `PortMeta::data_mode`** (body = `self.stream`, the port's **open-time** option): `{mode, list|binary}` never reaches `beam_io`, so a post-`open_port/2` change lives only in the `inet_opts` mirror and the rendering mode is resolved at the delivery arms from `PortRegistry::data_mode(port, packet.data_mode)`, the packet staying `Binary` at the drain boundary; do not add a second `PortMeta::data_mode` in `port.rs` (a duplicate inherent method is a compile error).
- **Delivery rides every point a driver callback can stage an active delivery**: `drain_active_inputs()` is called immediately after each of the six `drain_io_completions(...)` sites and the packets publish as `PortEvent::ActiveInput { packet }` on `pending_events`, so `has_nothing_to_poll` stays truthful without an arm of its own, and `PortEvent::port()` answers `None` for the variant so `close`'s purge can never re-attribute or drop a staged delivery.
  Two arms consume it: `Runtime::deliver_port_messages` (the scheduled `run` route) delivers to `packet.owner` through `deliver_in_table`, waking a parked receiver, and `RuntimeContext::poll_port_messages` (the `-eval` route) only when `packet.owner == self.pid()`.
- **Residual — `hsz > len`, a `beam_io` seam.** The C's list branch is `(mode == LIST) || (hsz > len)`, so a `{header,N}` wider than the payload is one flat string with no binary tail; `beam_io`'s `split_payload` clamps `hsz` and hands that case up as a whole-payload header with a present-but-empty body, rendering `[104|<<>>]`; fields keep their order and `[list]` mode is exact, every shape the e2e suite exercises has `hsz <= len`, and it is pinned at the renderer level (`active_input_tests.rs::a_header_as_wide_as_the_payload_keeps_an_empty_binary_tail`).
- **Measured difference from the oracle.** A write arriving while an `{active,once}`/`{active,N}` grant is spent: the C disarms the descriptor (`sock_select(desc, FD_READ, 0)`), so the *re-arm's* inline read delivers those bytes; `beam_io`'s poller stays armed, so `async_ready_input` harvests them into the port data queue instead; the suite asserts only what both sides agree on and prints the oracle's own differing value.
## `port/inet_opts.rs` — the accepted socket's inherited option block (A.4)
`prim_inet:open/6` opens every `inet` driver port with **one** option, `[binary]` (`erts/preloaded/src/prim_inet.erl:99-137`), and applies every other socket option afterwards through `setopts/2` — an `erlang:port_control/3` call carrying the C's `[opt_code, val_be32]` records — so neither `open_port/2`'s options nor `PortMeta::stream` know what the **driver** has been told.
`inet_opts.rs` is that knowledge, per port, keyed by **port number** inside `PortExtras` (`inet: InetOptions`) so `forget_port` drops it and a recycled number never inherits a dead socket's options.
**What is recorded**: every **successful** `setopts` argument verbatim and in arrival order (`PortInetOptions::records`, the replay source), the driver's current `{mode, list|binary}` (`PortInetOptions::mode`) and its `{exit_on_close,…}`.
One entry point records — `PortRegistry::note_inet_setopts(id, arg, ok)`, called from `src/port.rs::port_control` after the completion drain, gated on `inet_opts::is_setopts_control(op)` and `setopts_succeeded(reply)` — so a rejected `setopts` can never be inherited; the reply is read, never consumed, and the suites' own `setopts` helpers **detect** that hook (reading the recorded count across the control call), so they are no-ops on a wired runtime rather than double-recording (`accept_inherit_tests.rs::a_setopts_control_call_populates_the_option_mirror_from_production_alone`).
**The wire reader.** `walk` splits an argument into records while `rest.len() >= 5` (the C's `while (len >= 5)`, `inet_set_opts`, `inet_drv.c:6908`) and answers `None` if **any** byte is left over: the C silently ignores a partial trailing record because it has already applied what it consumed, but this walker's output is **replayed**, and replaying the subset that happens to parse could claim a configuration the listener does not have.
`record_len` is the C's per-code span: `linger` `RECORD+4`, `multicast_if` the whole `RECORD`, membership `RECORD+12` (IPv4) / `RECORD+20` (IPv6) and `None` for any other domain, `{active,N}` `RECORD+2`, `raw` `RECORD+8+arg_sz` with `arg_sz` at `RECORD+4..RECORD+8`, `netns`/`bind_to_device` `RECORD+value`.
**What is replayed onto an accepted port** — exactly the set `tcp_inet_copy` copies into the new descriptor (`inet_drv.c:11810-11827`), i.e. `is_inherited_record`'s 16 codes:
| code | record | C |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | `{recbuf, N}` — **raises** the copied `bufsz` while `INET_FLG_BUFFER_SET` is clear | `:7350-7360` |
| 20 | `{buffer, N}` — **assigns** the copied `bufsz` and sets that flag | `:7318-7330`, `:11820` |
| 21, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 30, 32, 35, 36, 37, 39, 49 | `nodelay`/`priority`/`tos`/`tclass`/`exit_on_close`/`reuseaddr`/`keepalive`/`sndbuf`/`delay_send`/`ipv6_v6only`/`bind_to_device`/`raw`/`netns`/`show_econnreset` — copied verbatim | `:11810-11827` |
`INET_OPT_RCVBUF = 7` sits beside `INET_LOPT_BUFFER = 20` in that table because the copied `inet.bufsz` has **two** writers, so a replay carrying only `{buffer,N}` would drop a listener configured by `{recbuf,N}` alone.
`accept_inherit_tests.rs::INHERITED_CODES` is the same 16 codes and asserts observable driver `getopts` agreement over every one of them, not replay shape.
## `port/peer_closed.rs` — the peer-close channel (`{tcp_closed, Port}`)
`beam_io::PortRegistry::port_closed(id)` is a **latched, sticky** observation that a connected socket's peer has closed; `peer_closed.rs` is the runtime half that turns it into the oracle's 2-tuple.
**The pieces.** `PeerCloseKind::Tcp` (`:129`) is the only kind: there is deliberately **no `udp_closed`**, because the C has no such atom — a connected UDP socket's ICMP failure is `{udp_error,S,econnrefused}`, a different family, so a `udp_inet` port never reports a peer close.
`PeerCloseReport { port, owner, kind, exit_on_close }` (`:151`) is the term-free drained report; `PeerCloseLatches` (`:184`, methods `is_reported`/`mark_reported`/`forget`/`prune`/`len`) is the once-per-descriptor latch set, keyed on *reported* so the sticky re-fire works; the latches live in `PortExtras::peer_closes` and are dropped by `forget_port`.
**Collection.** `collect_peer_closes` (`:283`) gates on `is_tcp_inet` over `driver_path_name`, so a `{spawn_driver,"tcp_inet"}` port and a spelled object path both resolve, and reads `registry.port_closed(id)`; an error or `false` forgets the latch, because a number `beam_io` recycled after an unseen close must start clean.
It then skips an already-reported number, gates on `inet_active`, reads `inet_exit_on_close`, marks reported, and pushes; `drain_peer_closes`/`drain_peer_closes_for(to)` (`:263`/`:277`) are the two wrappers over that one collect, the `-eval` form leaving a foreign recipient's latch **undrained** rather than dropping it (the once-only latch has no second chance), and `forget_peer_close(number)` (`:392`) is the idempotent per-number half.
**The gate.** `inet_active` (`:406`) is the port's **mirrored** `{active,…}` (`inet_opts::PortInetOptions::active_mode`), because `beam_io`'s socket driver holds the state machine but exposes no accessor while the runtime observes every accepted `{active,…}` record.
An **unknown mode is passive**, the C's own `inet_start` default (`inet_drv.c:10932`), because a fabricated `{tcp_closed,S}` is worse than none; a passive observation **keeps its latch**, so a later `{active,…}` makes the report due (the C's sticky arm, `inet_set_opts`, `inet_drv.c:7013-7021`).
`inet_exit_on_close` (`:415`) mirrors code 26 and defaults to `true` (`desc->inet.exitf = 1`, `inet_drv.c:10929`).
**Delivery, each half shaped like its `exit.rs` sibling.** `Runtime::deliver_peer_closes(&mut ProcessTable)` (`:454`) is the scheduler `run` route: it drains, skips an owner the table no longer holds (but still retires when `exit_on_close`), builds `peer_close_message` after the registry borrow drops, and delivers through `RuntimeContext::deliver_in_table` with the port as sender, so a receiver parked in `receive` is woken.
`RuntimeContext::poll_peer_closes()` (`:535`) is the `-eval` route: it takes `drain_peer_closes_for(self.pid())` and returns the messages inline, retiring when `exit_on_close`, so a foreign report stays latched for the scheduler route; `peer_close_message` (`:565`) interns `kind.tag()` (`"tcp_closed"`) in the runtime's **one shared** table and builds `terms_tuple(&[tag, report.port.to_eterm()])` on the pointer-stable arena.
**Retirement is `driver_exit`, not `port_close/1`.** `PortRegistry::retire_peer_closed(id)` (`:378`) mirrors `tcp_recv_closed`'s `if (desc->inet.exitf) driver_exit(desc->inet.port, 0)` (`inet_drv.c:12835-12837`), which runs `unlink_proc` **before** it delivers (`io.c:7244`): it refuses a port the runtime no longer holds (tested before the mark, so a refused retirement leaves none behind), sets `PortExtras::no_owner_exit` for that number, then calls the ordinary `close`, so the `'DOWN'` set is `signals_owner = false` — every monitor gets `{'DOWN', Ref, port, S, normal}` and a **trapping linked owner gets nothing**, measured on the oracle (`erlang:port_info(S, connected)` ⇒ `undefined` afterwards).
Those `'DOWN'` messages become deliverable at the port's close **report**, like every other close's signals, so the caller's route reports them one round later; `Runtime::retire_peer_closed_port(id)` (`:502`) skips a port already gone rather than folding that `badarg` into a delivery error.
**Owed (see Known Issues).** The two consuming call sites in `src/port.rs` — `deliver_port_messages` and `poll_port_messages` — are absent, so the channel is compiled, unit-pinned and unreachable end to end until they land.
**Residual.** A **spent** `{active,once}`/`{active,N}` grant is still mirrored as active (the transition happens inside the driver and no `{active,…}` record follows), so a close observed after a grant is exhausted reports where the C would stay silent until re-armed.
The channel's 12 cases are `peer_closed_tests.rs`: the active-mode and `exit_on_close` mirrors, the latch's recycle rule, the one-report-per-descriptor rule, the passive-then-armed re-fire, `udp_inet` reporting nothing, the tag atom, the `'DOWN'`-only retirement, both delivery routes, the foreign-latch rule, and one real loopback peer close end to end.
